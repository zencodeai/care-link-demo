# Actor-support agentic AI and mission-critical service requirements

Research/revision date: 2026-11-04. Status: proposed requirements extension. The intended service is mission-critical: clients need timely, appropriate, accessible support from accountable actors. The current deliverable remains a synthetic demonstrator of that service architecture. Demo status limits deployment and evidence claims; it must not reduce the intended service to a containment showcase without useful actor workflows.

## Purpose and place of agentic AI

Agentic AI supports actors in understanding a request, gathering permitted context, identifying missing information, finding verified service options, preparing an actionable plan, coordinating authorized work and checking the outcome. The human actor retains responsibility for clinical judgement and consequential service decisions. Deterministic services retain authority over access, consent, governance, commitments and execution. An agent is useful because it can adapt a bounded multi-step task to observations; it is not the owner of the client or the service.

An LLM producing a single summary is an AI assistance function. An **agentic workflow** iterates through a goal, scoped context, proposed next step, authorized tool call, observed result and updated task state until it completes, is cancelled or requires a human. Multiple named functions do not require multiple autonomous agents, separate models or a complex agent framework. The initial architecture can implement role-scoped assistants on one bounded runtime and the requested local Gemma model.

**TR-AI-01 — Service objective.** SHALL optimize the service process for client needs and preferences, accurate information, timely meaningful human connection, appropriate verified pathways, successful handoff and reduced actor administration, subject to safety, privacy, equity, security and governance constraints. “Optimal service” SHALL mean demonstrable improvement against an approved baseline and stated trade-offs, not a promise of mathematically optimal care. Engagement, throughput, model usage or police avoidance alone SHALL not define success.

## Similar systems and evidence

These are comparators for specific functions, not replicas, partners, endorsements or proof that autonomous crisis care works. None of the cited material establishes the safety of CARE-Link, local Gemma 3 4B IT or a multi-agent crisis platform. Imported service practices must not override the project’s no-police-integration constraint.

The recommended synthesis is **actor assistance plus bounded coordination**, with simulations for training and evaluation. It is not unattended AI counselling. Published usefulness of one assistance function cannot substitute for validation of another.

## Assistant responsibilities by actor

**TR-AI-02 — Explicit role-scoped assistance.** Each actor UI SHALL expose the applicable tasks below with visible scope, status, sources, uncertainty, approval requirements and cancel controls. Each task SHALL bind an initiating actor, client/session, purpose, permitted tools/data, budget and human owner. Actor login sessions remain separate from agent workload/task identities. No assistant inherits all privileges of its user or other assistants.

| Actor | Agentic assistance and permitted observation/action proposals | Human / deterministic authority retained |
|---|---|---|
| PID / client participant | Help express needs/preferences, organize a plan, retrieve approved information, compare verified options and explain next steps; direct low-risk navigation uses approved sources/templates | Client corrects, declines and chooses; clinical advice remains approved content/professional judgement; no diagnosis, inferred consent or hidden profiling |
| Carer | Organize attributed reports, identify missing information, find permitted support for the carer and prepare questions/context for a worker | Carer cannot authorize PID disclosure by relationship alone; disputes and private youth content remain protected |
| Professional participant / crisis worker | Prepare source-linked session timeline, missing/conflicting fact checklist, protocol retrieval, suggested wording, editable summary and referral package; monitor authorized task progress | Worker verifies clinically material facts, decides clinical next steps and approves final professional communications; model suggestions never become clinical facts automatically |
| Supervisor | Summarize queue/service status, flag overdue tasks, propose reassignment and handoff recovery, identify aggregate capacity concerns | Supervisor authorizes assignment/takeover; deterministic protocol controls priority; no individual performance/exposure ranking |
| Receiving service / navigator | Validate received package completeness, prepare clarification request, identify permitted logistical gaps and reconcile acknowledgement | Receiving actor accepts/rejects capacity and confirms receipt; assistant cannot promise acceptance, dispatch or ETA |
| Operator / security | Correlate approved health/security events, retrieve runbooks, draft incident timeline and propose bounded remediation | Deterministic controls perform preapproved containment; a human authorizes discretionary infrastructure changes/recovery; agent cannot edit live policy or secrets |
| Governance reviewer / auditor | Retrieve relevant rules, assemble traceable evidence, highlight missing approval/expired exception and draft an explanation | Human reviews/approves; deterministic gates enforce; assistant cannot certify compliance, clear findings or approve its own output |
| Trainer / role-player | Run explicitly fictional scenarios, vary scripted conditions and draft evidence-linked debrief | Human instructor validates feedback; no clinical competence certification from agent scores; no real personal story required |

**TR-AI-03 — Separate assistance modes.** SHALL distinguish: (a) automatic read-only assistance within scope, such as preparing a cited draft; (b) bounded execution of preauthorized low-risk operations, such as searching the synthetic directory; (c) proposed consequential work requiring current explicit human approval plus deterministic authorization, such as sending a consented simulator referral. Clinical judgement, consent grant, lawful disclosure, role/identity change, policy approval and production promotion cannot be delegated to the model. Existing deterministic hazard/escalation/queue protocols may execute without a model decision and must not wait for draft approval. Client-visible clinical/support content remains approved templated stub content in this demo; worker-generated free-text drafts require review before delivery.

## Bounded agent lifecycle and tools

**TR-AI-04 — Observable task state.** SHALL implement `requested → scoped → planning → awaiting_authorization / running → awaiting_observation / awaiting_human → completed / cancelled / failed`, with typed reasons and durable checkpoints. A plan contains structured steps and dependencies, not exposed hidden model reasoning. Track tool results, unresolved issues and explicit completion conditions; distinguish task completion from client outcome and successful care.

**TR-AI-05 — Goal and scope contract.** Before running SHALL create a task record with task/request ID, actor/workload identity, human owner, session/client, declared goal/purpose, input references and revisions, allowed tools/fields, approval mode, deadline/budgets, policy epoch, model/prompt versions and completion criteria. A model may propose a revised plan but cannot change goal, authority, data scope or budget without a newly authorized task revision. Every tool/subtask returns through the broker and current governance/security checks.

**TR-AI-06 — Grounded tools.** Initial tool candidates SHALL be narrow typed operations: read authorized session/plan fields, retrieve approved protocol, query verified directory, inspect authorized queue status, prepare an unsent draft, request human review, and check simulator receipt. Consent grant, diagnosis, emergency/police contact, arbitrary browsing/code execution and unrestricted database access are absent tools. A draft operation is not a send operation. Any allowed side-effect tool requires explicit capability, idempotency, state recheck and audit-before-effect under TR-GOV-06…12/TR-SEC-09…16.

**TR-AI-07 — Human review that matters.** Review UI SHALL show proposed changes, original attributed facts/source timestamps, recipient/fields/purpose, uncertainties and anticipated effects. Allow accept/edit/reject, not just blanket approval. Bind approval to exact task/payload/source revisions and expiry; material edits or stale facts invalidate approval and require rechecking. AI text SHALL remain visibly labelled until accepted, and accepted communications SHALL retain AI/human provenance. Human review SHALL not be treated as evidence of accuracy by itself; test reviewer detection of seeded consequential errors and automation bias.

**TR-AI-08 — Coordination without authority laundering.** If tasks delegate to specialist functions, preserve parent identity, purpose, same-or-narrower permissions, budgets and correlation; separate private actor contexts. Agent-to-agent output is untrusted and cannot create a consent/approval fact. No autonomous agent spawning or unconstrained tool chains. Implement specialization as modules first; introduce multi-agent execution only for a demonstrated workflow benefit with isolation/concurrency tests. This is a product-runtime requirement, not an instruction to spawn development agents.

## Mission-critical continuity and safety

**TR-AI-09 — Human service remains operable.** Actor-to-client messaging, human request/assignment/takeover, authorized basic record viewing and static information SHALL have a usable manual/deterministic path when AI is disabled, overloaded, wrong or unavailable. This preserves security and consent checks rather than bypassing them. A model failure SHALL not strand the client or silently drop a pending handoff. The worker may continue manually without accepting any AI suggestion. Offline fallback can show information but must not claim human connection when transport/staffing is unavailable.

**TR-AI-10 — Safe proactive assistance.** Agents MAY identify an overdue authorized task, missing fact or changed service state and propose next steps. They SHALL not silently change priority, infer a diagnosis, make external contact, send reminders without consent or promise staffing/availability. Notify the responsible actor with source evidence and uncertainty. Rate-limit suggestions and allow dismissal without suppressing mandatory deterministic escalation. Wait/silence alone never authorizes session termination or emergency action.

**TR-AI-11 — Hazards and operating assurance.** For each assistance function SHALL document harmful commission/omission, hallucination, misleading certainty, stale context, conflicting reports, automation bias, alert fatigue, translation errors, privacy leakage, malicious instruction, duplicated action and model outage. Link hazards to preventive control, recovery path, responsible actor and negative scenario. Do not claim that deterministic keyword screening is a validated clinical hazard detector. A missed ambiguous hazard SHALL be an explicit evaluation case; clinical feature/triage deployment remains blocked until reviewed. Mission-critical availability targets require validated staffing, redundancy and recovery; the single-host demo cannot establish them.

## Worked actor-support scenario

**TR-AI-12 — Demonstrate end-to-end value.** A synthetic carer requests help; deterministic intake handles disclosure and human request. A scoped assistant proposes missing questions and retrieves permitted plan fields and fictional directory options. A worker receives an attributed summary, missing/conflicting facts and sourced options. The worker edits/rejects suggestions, chooses a pathway with the client, and approves an exact consented simulator referral. Deterministic controls authorize dispatch; the assistant checks receipt, observes timeout/rejection and proposes an alternative without inventing a successful handoff. The worker remains in control, and a manual path continues if the model fails. Every stage SHALL be visible and auditable in the respective actor UI. A chat-only demo does not satisfy this requirement.

## Evaluation and acceptance

**TR-AI-13 — Demonstrate useful assistance without unapproved trade-offs.** Compare actor-only and actor-plus-AI work on matched synthetic cases, with counterbalanced order and independently reviewed rubrics. Measure time to a verified actionable option and meaningful human connection; critical fact accuracy/omission; unsupported claims; appropriate handoff/consent; actor editing/rejection and cognitive workload; client agency/accessibility; EN/FR differences; security/governance violations; manual continuity under failure. Include difficult, ambiguous and adversarial multi-turn cases. Report raw counts, missingness and latency/failure distributions. No throughput benefit compensates for an unauthorized action or missed critical scenario. Synthetic evaluation does not establish real clinical benefit or a non-inferiority margin.

**TR-AI-14 — Capability admission by task.** The required LM Studio/Gemma model SHALL pass each proposed task’s structured-output, groundedness, context, EN/FR, latency and adversarial evaluation before enabling that task. No assumption that a 4B model can safely plan every workflow. Keep unsupported functions manual, simplify their scope or use deterministic templates; do not silently switch models or infer clinical competence from fluent text. Proposed task usefulness targets require a measured actor-only baseline; initially require zero seeded critical-fact fabrication/omission and zero unauthorized effects in approved acceptance scenarios. Failing a scenario blocks that function until resolved/reviewed.

**TR-AI-15 — Versioned lifecycle and ownership.** Each assistant function SHALL have an intended use, named responsible human role, enabled modes/tools, limits, evaluation suite, approved model/prompt/config versions and rollback/disable path. Offline changes and shadow evaluations SHALL not affect live decisions. Worker suggestion acceptance, incident feedback or client conversations SHALL not trigger online learning, fine-tuning or self-modification. Reflect approved changes in the governance register, not just a prompt file.

These requirements extend TR-SCP-02, TR-ACT-01…03, TR-WF-01…09 and TR-LLM-01…05. They preserve authority/security constraints while making useful actor assistance a primary delivery outcome. They do not close upstream clinical/legal questions or authorize real crisis use.

> This prototype demonstrates control and containment architecture using non-clinical stub logic and synthetic data. It provides no evidence of clinical safety, triage validity, or equity performance, and must not be cited as such.
